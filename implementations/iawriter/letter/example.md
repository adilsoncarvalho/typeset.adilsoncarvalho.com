Dear Registrar,

I am writing about the setting of the Institute's annual report, which arrived this morning and which I read with rather more attention to its margins than to its contents. I hope that is taken in the spirit intended.

The text column runs to something near a hundred and ten characters.[^Measured on page four, between the outer margins: 168mm at a 10pt body, which is about 112 characters.] By the second page I had lost my place twice, and by the fourth I had stopped reading and started measuring. The report itself is careful work; it deserves better than a layout that makes it tiring to read.

This is a cheap problem to fix. Narrowing the column to sixty-six characters costs one afternoon and no money, and the space it frees on the right can carry the marginal notes that are currently crowded into footnotes.

The arithmetic, for the three sizes you might reasonably use:

| Base size | Characters per line | Readable |
| --------- | ------------------: | :------- |
| 10pt      |                 112 | no       |
| 11pt      |                  66 | yes      |
| 12pt      |                  61 | yes      |

Your own house style says it best:

> A line the eye cannot return from is a line the reader does not read twice.

If it would be useful, I am glad to set one section of next year's report as a demonstration.

Yours sincerely,

Adilson Carvalho

Enc. typeset.css; two specimen pages

P.S. The tables were excellent — tabular figures throughout, which is more than most annual reports manage.

Drop cap

Three lines deep, serif, in the accent colour — for the opening of an essay or a chapter, and nowhere else.

There is nothing especially modern about wanting a document to look considered. The drop cap descends from the illuminated initial, which existed for a practical reason: in a manuscript with no title page and no table of contents, a large decorated letter was the only way to tell the reader that something had begun.

The practical reason is gone. The signal is not.

Opening words

The first few words in small caps, so the jump from a 33pt initial back to 11pt body text has a step in between rather than a cliff.

There is nothing especially modern about wanting a document to look considered — the small-caps run carries the eye back down to body size before the paragraph continues.

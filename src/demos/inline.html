1 · Bold

Weight, not italics, for the sentence's own load-bearing clause — the thing a skimming reader must not miss.

The deadline is Friday, not Monday, whatever the earlier email said.

2 · Italic

For a title or a term of art — not a general-purpose emphasis dial reached for whenever a word feels important.

She is still reading Typographie, Ruder's own case for restraint.

3 · Nested italic

Emphasis inside emphasis flips back to roman rather than compounding into a heavier italic — correct behaviour, and something most engines get wrong by default.

The review called it the definitive account — definitive, not merely thorough — of the period.

4 · Small caps

For named entities, acronyms and stage directions — never a whole sentence shouted in capitals.

The Bauhaus closed its doors in 1933.

5 · Abbreviation

Small caps and a dotted underline mark a term the reader can expand. The expansion itself lives in the title attribute, for a screen reader or a hovering pointer — paper offers neither, so the mark is what a printed page keeps.

Exported as a PDF, the file survives whatever the recipient has installed.

6 · Superscript

Raised, without opening up the line's own leading — a reference mark must not stretch the line it sits in.

The claim is contested,3 and the footnote says why.

7 · Subscript

The same rule below the baseline — a formula's line keeps exactly the surrounding paragraph's height.

Water is H2O, nothing more exotic.

8 · Deletion

A struck, muted run for text a revision removes — present in the document, marked as gone rather than cut.

Delivery takes ten business days.

9 · Insertion

An underlined run for text a revision adds — the tracked-changes counterpart to deletion, meant to sit beside it rather than replace it silently.

Delivery takes ten five business days.

10 · Highlight

A wash behind the glyphs, bleeding slightly beyond them, for one phrase the reader's eye should catch first — not a device for a whole paragraph.

The clause that actually changes the deadline is buried in paragraph four.

11 · Key

A drawn keycap for a literal keystroke — the one inline style with its own border, because a key is an object on a keyboard, not a word to emphasise.

Save early: a document is one ⌘S away from being backed up.

1 · Unordered list

A grey mid-dot marker, weighted so it reads as a mark rather than competing with the words beside it. A runover line aligns to the text, never to the marker.

  • An unordered item.
  • A second item, long enough to wrap onto a second line so you can see that the runover aligns to the text, not to the marker.

2 · Unordered list, nested

The nested marker switches to an en dash, lighter in weight than the level above it — two levels deep is the limit worth designing for.

  • A first-level item, followed by a nested list:
    • The nested marker is an en dash.
    • Two levels is the limit worth designing for.

3 · Ordered list

Tabular lining figures, so a run past nine keeps every marker in the same column rather than drifting once a second digit appears.

  1. First item.
  2. Second item.
  3. Third item.
  4. Fourth item.
  5. Fifth item.
  6. Sixth item.
  7. Seventh item.
  8. Eighth item.
  9. Ninth item.
  10. Tenth item — the second digit arrives here, and the marker still sits in the column the single digits held.
  11. Eleventh item.

4 · Ordered list, nested

Sub-items restart the counter and switch to lower alpha — the same demotion the nested unordered marker makes.

  1. A first-level item, followed by sub-items:
    1. First sub-item.
    2. Second sub-item.

5 · Tight list

No space between items — for an enumeration of values, not a sequence of sentences a reader pauses between.

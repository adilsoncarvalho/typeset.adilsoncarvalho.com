1 · Letter page

A letter's own page, beside the default one: 32mm at the head — the standard margin plus a 12mm letterhead band, so the sender block sits where an envelope window expects it — 20mm at the sides, 20mm at the foot: the same as any other page, because nothing in a letter's own principles calls for either to differ. No running head and no folio, which is what the empty head and foot of the right-hand sheet show. The markup beside this pane is the whole of it: the class goes on the document element, and every pane below carries it.

2 · Sender block

The name line in small caps at body size, the lines beneath it a step smaller — a hierarchy of data, not decoration. Never bold and never larger: that turns a letter into stationery. Leave out what a letter does not need: no email address, no telephone number, unless the correspondence calls for one.

3 · Address block

Line-broken data, not prose — the recipient's address exactly as it would sit on an envelope: the name in small caps at body size, the rest a step smaller, tighter leading than the body, never justified, and the line breaks as authored.

4 · Address label

The caption that says whose address this is: sans, uppercase, tracked open and faint, on a line of its own above the block. It never appears outside an address block — but it is set by eight declarations of its own, and the address beneath it shares none of them.

5 · Address column

The sender and recipient blocks together, held at the standard margin from the paper's edge. Where the text sits further in — this page has the wide margin — the column reaches out by the difference, so the letterhead stays where an envelope window expects it. Where the text is nearer the paper than the standard margin, the column stays with the text.

6 · Crest

Optional: a crest or monogram in the top right corner, 20mm wide, held at the standard margin from the paper's right edge by the same rule as the address column. A mark drawn in one ink takes the accent. A letter without one leaves the corner empty.

7 · Date line

Oldstyle figures, flush left — the same numeral convention the rest of the document already sets, not a date format of its own. Three lines of space above it set the letterhead off from the letter.

27 August 2026

8 · Line under the date

A dedication, a feast, a devotion — whatever the writer puts under the date. Italic, muted and a step smaller, on the date's own next line, so the two read as one block.

9 · Salutation

A greeting, set like any other paragraph in the letter — no weight change, no size change, no heading trying to happen — with two lines of room above and below it.

10 · Quotation — in a letter

A prayer, a line, a remembered phrase: indented on both sides, italic and muted, with no rule. A rule is the essay's quotation; a letter sets its quotations apart by indent and colour. Inside a letter, every block quote takes this form.

That was my prayer while I waited.

Forgive me my failure to act, O Lord, and if I miss confession today, I will take that as a warning.

The queue moved at a glacial pace.

11 · Footnote — in a letter

The same marker as the rest of the document's apparatus — superscript, no brackets. On a one-page letter, the rule and the numbered note it sits above are the page foot in every sense but name.

Dear Registrar,

I am writing about the setting of the Institute’s annual report, which arrived this morning and which I read with more attention to its margins than to its contents. I hope that is taken in the spirit intended.

The text column runs to something near a hundred and ten characters. By the second page I had lost my place twice, and by the fourth I had stopped reading and started measuring.

This is a cheap problem to fix, and I would be glad to send the specification I use for my own documents.

  1. Measured on page four, between the outer margins: 168mm at a 10pt body, which is about 112 characters.

12 · Closing

The same plain rhythm as the salutation it echoes — the only difference is the room around it: three lines above, two below, not a style of its own.

Yours sincerely,

13 · Signature block

The typed name, with room above it to sign. No ruled line: a rule above a name is a form to be filled in, and this is not a form.

Yours sincerely,

Adilson Carvalho

14 · Enclosures

Enc. introduces a sentence; it does not head a section, so it carries no small caps and no weight change of its own.

15 · Postscript

A postscript is a sentence that happens to begin with P.S. — the label is not a heading, so it matches the text it introduces exactly. It stands off what precedes it by the signature's own three lines.

Enc. typeset.css; two specimen pages

P.S. The tables were excellent — tabular figures throughout, which is more than most annual reports manage.

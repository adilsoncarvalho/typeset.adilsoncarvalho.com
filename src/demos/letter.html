1 · Letter page

A letter's own page, beside the default one: 32mm at the head so the sender block sits where an envelope window expects it, 25mm at the sides, 28mm at the foot — and no running head and no folio, which is what the empty head and foot of the right-hand sheet show. The markup beside this pane is the whole of it: the class goes on the document element, and every pane below carries it.

2 · Sender block

One style throughout, including the name — bold or larger turns a letter into stationery. Leave out what a letter does not need: no email address, no telephone number, unless the correspondence calls for one.

3 · Address block

Line-broken data, not prose — the recipient's address exactly as it would sit on an envelope: tighter leading than the body, never justified, and the line breaks as authored.

4 · Address label

The caption that says whose address this is: sans, uppercase, tracked open and faint, on a line of its own above the block. It never appears outside an address block — but it is set by eight declarations of its own, and the address beneath it shares none of them.

5 · Date line

Oldstyle figures, flush left — the same numeral convention the rest of the document already sets, not a date format of its own.

6 · Line under the date

A dedication, a feast, a devotion — whatever the writer puts under the date. Italic, at body size, close enough beneath it that the two read as one line rather than two.

7 · Salutation

A greeting, set exactly like any other paragraph in the letter — no weight change, no size change, no heading trying to happen.

Dear Registrar,

8 · Footnote — in a letter

The same marker as the rest of the document's apparatus — superscript, no brackets. On a one-page letter, the rule and the numbered note it sits above are the page foot in every sense but name.

Dear Registrar,

I am writing about the setting of the Institute’s annual report, which arrived this morning and which I read with more attention to its margins than to its contents. I hope that is taken in the spirit intended.

The text column runs to something near a hundred and ten characters. By the second page I had lost my place twice, and by the fourth I had stopped reading and started measuring.

This is a cheap problem to fix, and I would be glad to send the specification I use for my own documents.

  1. Measured on page four, between the outer margins: 168mm at a 10pt body, which is about 112 characters.

9 · Closing

The same plain rhythm as the salutation it echoes — the only difference is the room above it, not a style of its own.

Yours sincerely,

10 · Signature block

The typed name, with room above it to sign. No ruled line: a rule above a name is a form to be filled in, and this is not a form.

Yours sincerely,

Adilson Carvalho

11 · Enclosures

Enc. introduces a sentence; it does not head a section, so it carries no small caps and no weight change of its own.

Adilson Carvalho

12 · Postscript

A postscript is a sentence that happens to begin with P.S. — the label is not a heading, so it matches the text it introduces exactly.

Enc. typeset.css; two specimen pages
